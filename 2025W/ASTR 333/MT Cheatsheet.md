## Midterm Study Guide: Astrobiology and Planetary Science

### A Little Bit About Bio

**Define life and critique your definition**
* **Answer:** Life is often defined as a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution (NASA definition). 
* **Critique:** This definition is functional rather than descriptive. It excludes individual organisms that cannot reproduce (like mules) and requires a long-term observation of a population to confirm "evolution."

**What do we think is necessary for life?**
* **Answer:** Thermodynamic disequilibrium (energy source), a liquid medium (like water), and raw materials (CHNOPS: Carbon, Hydrogen, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Phosphorus, Sulfur).

**What properties of water make it an ideal medium for life (we think)?**
* **Answer:** It is a polar solvent (dissolves many substances), remains liquid over a wide temperature range, and its solid form (ice) is less dense than its liquid form, providing insulation for sub-surface environments.

**What are properties of carbon that make it so useful for life (we think)?**
* **Answer:** Carbon can form four stable covalent bonds, allowing for the creation of complex, diverse, and stable long-chain molecules (polymers) and rings.

**What is Darwinian evolution? Why is it incorporated into the definition of life?**
* **Answer:** It is the process of heritable variation and natural selection. It is included because it allows life to adapt to changing environments and increase complexity over time, distinguishing it from simple chemistry.

**Why is a virus not considered to be life?**
* **Answer:** Viruses are not self-sustaining; they cannot replicate or undergo metabolism without a host cell's machinery.

**What is an example of an energy source that is NOT sunlight?**
* **Answer:** Chemosynthesis (using chemical energy from redox reactions, such as those at hydrothermal vents).

**What are the most abundant elements in the Solar System?**
* **Answer:** Hydrogen (approx. 74%) and Helium (approx. 24%) by mass.

**Helium is the second most abundant element in the Solar System. Why is it not abundant in life?**
* **Answer:** Helium is a noble gas; it is chemically inert and does not form the bonds necessary for complex molecules.

**Can you convert between abundance by number and abundance by mass?**
* **Answer:** Yes. Multiply the number of atoms by their respective atomic masses to get the mass ratio.
    $Mass\_Fraction_A = \frac{N_A \cdot m_A}{\sum (N_i \cdot m_i)}$

**What are hydrogen bonds and how are they different from covalent bonds?**
* **Answer:** Covalent bonds involve the *sharing* of electrons between atoms within a molecule. Hydrogen bonds are weaker electrostatic attractions between the partial positive charge of a hydrogen atom in one molecule and the partial negative charge of another atom (like Oxygen) in a different molecule.

**Describe the significance of hydrophilic and hydrophobic. What is interesting about lipids in this regard?**
* **Answer:** Hydrophilic ("water-loving") substances are polar and dissolve in water. Hydrophobic ("water-fearing") substances are non-polar and repel water. Lipids are **amphiphilic**, meaning they have a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail, which allows them to form membranes.

**What are major macromolecules for life?**
* **Answer:** Proteins, Carbohydrates (sugars), Lipids, and Nucleic acids (DNA/RNA).

**Why are amino acids important for life? What is the amine and carboxyl?**
* **Answer:** They are the building blocks of proteins. The **amine group** is $-NH_2$ and the **carboxyl group** is $-COOH$.

**Where does ammonia fit into our understanding of life?**
* **Answer:** Ammonia ($NH_3$) is a source of nitrogen for amino acids and nucleic acids. It is also considered a potential alternative liquid medium for life on very cold worlds.

**Can you recognize a lipid, sugar, amino acid, and nucleic acid?**
* **Answer:** * **Lipid:** Long hydrocarbon chains.
    * **Sugar:** Ring structures with a 1:2:1 ratio of C:H:O.
    * **Amino Acid:** Central Carbon, amine group, carboxyl group, and an R-side chain.
    * **Nucleic Acid:** Phosphate group, sugar, and a nitrogenous base.

**How does water shape sugars?**
* **Answer:** In aqueous solutions, long-chain sugars often fold into stable ring structures.

**What is the role of water in polymerization?**
* **Answer:** Most biological polymerization occurs via **dehydration synthesis**, where a water molecule is removed to link monomers together.

**What is a peptide?**
* **Answer:** A short chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

**What property of nucleobases plays a major role in enabling them to be copied? What are the different bases?**
* **Answer:** **Complementary base pairing** via hydrogen bonding. Bases: Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), Thymine (T) in DNA, and Uracil (U) in RNA.

**What is a messenger RNA? What is the difference in bases?**
* **Answer:** mRNA carries genetic information from DNA to the ribosome for protein synthesis. RNA uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T).

**What is translation? Transcription?**
* **Answer:** * **Transcription:** DNA is copied into mRNA.
    * **Translation:** mRNA is decoded to build a protein.

**Are organic molecules found in interstellar space? What are some examples?**
* **Answer:** Yes. Examples include formaldehyde, methanol, and complex polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs).

**What does the Murchison meteorite tell us about biology?**
* **Answer:** It contains dozens of amino acids, suggesting that the building blocks of life can form abiotically in space.

**What are biosignatures/biomarkers? Give some examples.**
* **Answer:** Evidence of past or present life. Examples: Atmospheric oxygen/methane disequilibrium, fossilized stromatolites, or specific isotopic ratios (e.g., $^{12}C$ enrichment).

**What is the red edge?**
* **Answer:** A sharp increase in the reflectance of vegetation in the near-infrared spectrum, used as a potential biosignature for chlorophyll-based life.

**What is the importance of stable isotope ratios in the context of astrobiology?**
* **Answer:** Biological processes often prefer lighter isotopes (like $^{12}C$ over $^{13}C$). A depletion of $^{13}C$ in organic matter can be a signature of biological metabolism.

**What is a stromatolite?**
* **Answer:** Layered sedimentary structures created by the trapping and binding of sediment by microbial mats (primarily cyanobacteria).

**Compare the atmospheres of Venus, Mars, and Earth. Why is Earth's atmospheric composition so different?**
* **Answer:** Venus and Mars are $CO_2$ dominated. Earth's atmosphere is $N_2$ and $O_2$ dominated because of biology (photosynthesis producing $O_2$) and the liquid water cycle (sequestering $CO_2$ into rocks).

### A Little Bit About Planets

**What is the IAU definition of a planet?**
* **Answer:** 1) Orbits the Sun, 2) Is nearly spherical (hydrostatic equilibrium), and 3) Has cleared the neighborhood around its orbit.

**How are we defining planet in the class?**
* **Answer:** Generally any object large enough for gravity to make it round, potentially including large moons (geological definition).

**Give the order of the planets. Also describe where the main asteroid belt is located, the Kuiper belt, and the Oort cloud. Also, what are centaurs?**
* **Answer:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. 
    * **Asteroid Belt:** Between Mars and Jupiter.
    * **Kuiper Belt:** Beyond Neptune.
    * **Oort Cloud:** Spherical shell at the very edge of the Solar System.
    * **Centaurs:** Small bodies that orbit between the giant planets (cross the orbits of giants).

**What are ice giants, gas giants, and terrestrial planets?**
* **Answer:** * **Terrestrial:** Small, rocky (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars).
    * **Gas Giants:** Massive, H/He dominated (Jupiter, Saturn).
    * **Ice Giants:** Significant "icy" mantles of water, ammonia, methane (Uranus, Neptune).

**What are some key properties of the planets? Which planets have moons?**
* **Answer:** All planets except Mercury and Venus have moons. Jupiter and Saturn have the most.

**What is so peculiar about Earth's moon? What is a criterion used in class to distinguish the Moon from a planet?**
* **Answer:** It is very large relative to its host planet. A criterion used is the **location of the barycenter** (though for Earth-Moon, it is inside Earth) or the ability to clear an orbit.

**What are the internal layers of the gas giants vs the ice giants?**
* **Answer:** * **Gas Giants:** Molecular H, Metallic H core.
    * **Ice Giants:** Molecular H/He envelope, "slushy" water/ammonia mantle, rocky core.

**How do we know the surface of Venus so well?**
* **Answer:** Radar mapping (Magellan mission) because the clouds are opaque to visible light.

**On planets, especially Earth, what is used to define the different atmosphere layers?**
* **Answer:** The temperature gradient (whether temperature increases or decreases with altitude).

**What are the atmospheric layers on Earth? What is a way to "see" the tropopause through thunderstorms?**
* **Answer:** Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere. "Anvil" clouds in thunderstorms mark the tropopause because the rising air hits the stable stratosphere and spreads out.

**What causes the stratosphere and thermosphere to have the temperature structure they have?**
* **Answer:** * **Stratosphere:** Heating by UV absorption by Ozone ($O_3$).
    * **Thermosphere:** Heating by high-energy X-rays and UV from the Sun.

**What is hydrostatic equilibrium and what does it imply for atmospheric pressure as a function of altitude?**
* **Answer:** A balance between the upward pressure gradient force and the downward force of gravity. It implies pressure decreases exponentially with altitude.

**How much water is in Earth's hydrosphere as a fraction of Earth's mass?**
* **Answer:** Very little, approximately 0.023%.

**Compare and contrast Earth's atmospheric layers with those of Venus and Mars. Which layer is "missing" on Venus and Mars?**
* **Answer:** Venus and Mars lack a **stratosphere** because they lack an ozone layer to cause a temperature inversion.

**What are some of the cloud types on the giant planets? Do they have tropospheres and stratospheres? How do we define a reference altitude?**
* **Answer:** Ammonia, Ammonium hydrosulfide, and Water ice clouds. They have tropospheres and stratospheres. Reference altitude is usually defined where pressure is 1 bar.

**Which planets have rings?**
* **Answer:** All four giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).

**Are moons always smaller than planets? Discuss.**
* **Answer:** No. Ganymede and Titan are larger than the planet Mercury, though less massive.

**What is the significance of c-type asteroids? What are some other asteroid types?**
* **Answer:** C-type (Carbonaceous) are the most common and contain organic compounds/water. Other types: S-type (Stony/Silicate), M-type (Metallic).

**What are near-Earth asteroids and what are the various classifications?**
* **Answer:** Asteroids with orbits that bring them close to Earth. Classifications: Amors, Apollos, Atens, and Atiras based on their perihelion and semi-major axis.

**What is an asteroid vs a comet?**
* **Answer:** Asteroids are primarily rocky/metallic; comets have significant volatile ices (water, $CO_2$) that sublimate to form a coma/tail.

**How many tails does a comet get, and when do comets "grow" tails?**
* **Answer:** Two (usually): a Dust tail and an Ion (Gas) tail. They grow tails when they get close enough to the Sun for ices to sublimate.

**What is the difference between long and short-period comets?**
* **Answer:** Short-period ($<200$ years) originate from the Kuiper Belt; Long-period ($>200$ years) originate from the Oort Cloud.

### A Little Bit About Exoplanets

**About how many confirmed exoplanets are there? What does this mean for exoplanet abundance in the Galaxy?**
* **Answer:** Over 5,000. It implies that planets are common and there are likely more planets than stars in our galaxy.

**What are some exoplanet types that do not exist in the Solar System?**
* **Answer:** Hot Jupiters, Super-Earths, and Mini-Neptunes.

**What is the hot Neptune desert?**
* **Answer:** A region close to stars where very few Neptune-sized planets are found; they likely lose their atmospheres due to intense radiation.

**We have not found a true Solar System analogue yet. Is that peculiar? Does it mean we are outliers?**
* **Answer:** It is likely a **selection effect**. Our current methods (transit, radial velocity) are most sensitive to large planets close to their stars. We haven't been looking long enough to easily see Earth-sized planets at 1 AU.

**Are there multiplanet systems known?**
* **Answer:** Yes, hundreds (e.g., TRAPPIST-1).

**What is the closest exoplanet to the Solar System?**
* **Answer:** Proxima Centauri b.

### Toward Complexity

**What is the earliest record of life on Earth and what are some of the nuances? How does early life correlate with cratering rates on the Moon?**
* **Answer:** ~3.5 to 3.8 billion years ago (stromatolites and isotopic signatures). This roughly coincides with the end of the **Late Heavy Bombardment**, suggesting life emerged as soon as the environment became stable.

**Be able to calculate energies, speeds, etc. associated with asteroid impacts.**
* **Answer:** Kinetic Energy: $KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$. 
    * 1 ton of TNT $\approx 4.18 \times 10^9$ J.

**Why are biological cells so important?**
* **Answer:** They provide an enclosed environment to concentrate chemicals, protect from the outside, and maintain disequilibrium.

**What are basic components of cells? What is the difference between a eukaryote and a prokaryote?**
* **Answer:** Components: Membrane, genetic material, cytoplasm, ribosomes. 
    * **Prokaryotes:** No nucleus (Bacteria, Archaea).
    * **Eukaryotes:** Have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

**What is molecular chirality and how is it important to life?**
* **Answer:** "Handedness" of molecules. Life on Earth is **homochiral**: it uses only L-amino acids and D-sugars.

**What is the big puzzle about chirality and life?**
* **Answer:** Abiotic chemistry produces a 50/50 **racemic mixture**, but life uses only one version. We don't know why or how this symmetry was broken.

**What is an enantiomer, isomer?**
* **Answer:** * **Isomer:** Same formula, different arrangement.
    * **Enantiomer:** Mirror-image isomers (like hands).

**What is meant by a racemic mixture?**
* **Answer:** An equal 50/50 mix of left- and right-handed enantiomers.

**What are possible origins of chirality?**
* **Answer:** Circularly polarized light in space, mineral surface templates, or random chance amplified by self-replication.

**What is meant by circular polarized light and might it be important?**
* **Answer:** Light where the electric field rotates. It can preferentially destroy one enantiomer over another, potentially seeding homochirality in space.

**How might rocks be important to the emergence of life?**
* **Answer:** Minerals can act as catalysts and templates for organizing organic molecules.

**In terms of chirality, what is special about meteorites?**
* **Answer:** Some (like Murchison) show a slight excess of L-amino acids, supporting an extra-terrestrial origin for chirality.

**Discuss the Miller-Urey experiment.**
* **Answer:** It showed that discharging sparks (lightning) through a reducing atmosphere ($CH_4, NH_3, H_2, H_2O$) could produce amino acids abiotically.

**Which Solar System moon is of particular interest for the emergence of life? Why?**
* **Answer:** Enceladus or Europa (due to subsurface liquid water oceans and hydrothermal activity).

**What role might lipids have played in the emergence of life?**
* **Answer:** They spontaneously form membranes (vesicles), creating the first "proto-cells."

**What is a lipid bilayer? micelle, liposome, vesicle?**
* **Answer:** * **Micelle:** Sphere with tails in, heads out.
    * **Bilayer:** Two layers of lipids with tails facing each other.
    * **Vesicle/Liposome:** A hollow sphere made of a lipid bilayer.

**Can proteins also play an important role in cell development? If so, how?**
* **Answer:** Yes, as catalysts (enzymes) and structural components.

**What are black smokers?**
* **Answer:** Hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor that emit mineral-rich, superheated water.

**What is chemosynthesis and how is it different from photosynthesis? Give a reaction.**
* **Answer:** Chemosynthesis uses chemical energy instead of light. 
    * Example: $CO_2 + 4H_2 \rightarrow CH_4 + 2H_2O + Energy$.

**Discuss RNA and how it could be a solution to the chicken and egg problem.**
* **Answer:** **RNA World Hypothesis**: RNA can both store genetic info (like DNA) and catalyze reactions (like proteins/enzymes), meaning it could have preceded both.

**What is evidence that life emerged in very hot conditions?**
* **Answer:** The "root" of the tree of life is dominated by **hyperthermophiles** (organisms that love extreme heat).

**What is meant by panspermia? What was the importance of the Allan Hills Martian meteorite?**
* **Answer:** The idea that life (or its building blocks) travels between planets via meteorites. **ALH84001** (Allan Hills) was claimed to have microfossils from Mars, sparking massive interest in astrobiology, though the claim remains controversial.

**What is the importance of the ice/snow/frost line in the Solar Nebula?**
* **Answer:** The distance where it is cold enough for volatiles (water, $CO_2$) to freeze. Inside the line, only rocky planets form; outside, giant planets can grow from icy cores.

**When an interstellar cloud of gas and dust collapses, why does it form a disc?**
* **Answer:** Conservation of angular momentum. As the cloud shrinks, it spins faster, and centrifugal forces prevent collapse perpendicular to the axis of rotation, flattening it.

### Kepler & Physics

**What are Kepler's three laws?**
1.  Orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
2.  A line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times.
3.  The square of the period ($P^2$) is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis ($a^3$).

**What are the names of the components of an ellipse?**
* **Answer:** Major axis, semi-major axis ($a$), semi-minor axis ($b$), foci, and eccentricity ($e$).

**What is pericentre, apocentre? What is eccentricity?**
* **Answer:** * **Pericentre:** Closest point to the focus.
    * **Apocentre:** Furthest point.
    * **Eccentricity:** Measure of how "stretched" the ellipse is (0 = circle).

**What is the distinction between a scalar and vector?**
* **Answer:** Scalar has magnitude only (mass, temperature); vector has magnitude and direction (velocity, force).

**What is Newton's law of gravity?**
* **Answer:** $F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$.

**What is an orbit?**
* **Answer:** The path of an object under the influence of gravity; essentially "falling" around another body without hitting it.

**What is Newton's general form of Kepler's third law?**
* **Answer:** $P^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{G(M_1+M_2)} a^3$.

**For two objects orbiting each other, what is the barycentre?**
* **Answer:** The common center of mass. Both objects orbit the barycenter. The period is the same for both, but the orbital radius depends on their mass ratio.

**What is the circular speed, escape speed?**
* **Answer:** * **Circular speed:** $v_c = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$
    * **Escape speed:** $v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$

**What are different forms of energy?**
* **Answer:** Kinetic ($1/2 mv^2$), Gravitational Potential ($-GMm/r$), Thermal, Radiative.

**How are kinetic and gravitational energy related to an orbit?**
* **Answer:** Total energy $E = KE + PE$. For a bound orbit, $E$ is negative.

### Irradiation and Atmospheres

**What is luminosity? What is flux?**
* **Answer:** * **Luminosity ($L$):** Total energy emitted per second (Watts).
    * **Flux ($F$):** Energy received per unit area ($F = L / 4\pi d^2$).

**How do stellar sizes/luminosity change with mass?**
* **Answer:** Massive stars are much larger and exponentially more luminous ($L \propto M^{3.5}$).

**What is a black body?**
* **Answer:** An idealized object that absorbs all radiation and re-emits it perfectly based on its temperature.

**How does intensity and peak radiation change with temperature?**
* **Answer:** As $T$ increases, total intensity increases ($T^4$) and peak wavelength shifts to shorter (bluer) wavelengths.

**What is Wien's law?**
* **Answer:** $\lambda_{max} = \frac{2.9 \times 10^{-3}}{T}$ (Peak wavelength is inversely proportional to temperature).

**What is the Stefan-Boltzmann law?**
* **Answer:** $F = \sigma T^4$.

**What is the Bond albedo ($A$)?**
* **Answer:** The fraction of total incident sunlight reflected back into space.

**Determine the temperature of a planet ($T_p$):**
* **Answer:** $T_p = \left[ \frac{L(1-A)}{16\pi \sigma d^2} \right]^{1/4}$

**Why do terrestrial planets diverge from that calculation?**
* **Answer:** The Greenhouse Effect (atmospheres trap heat).

**What is the habitable zone?**
* **Answer:** The region around a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface. Nuances: Depends on atmospheric composition and planet mass.

**How are frequency and wavelength connected?**
* **Answer:** $c = \lambda \nu$ (speed of light = wavelength $\times$ frequency).

### Early Earth

**What are major geological eras?**
* **Answer:** Hadean (hellish), Archean (first life), Proterozoic (oxygenation), Phanerozoic (visible life/now).

**How did the Moon form?**
* **Answer:** Giant Impact Hypothesis: A Mars-sized body (Theia) struck the proto-Earth.

**How did Earth's interior layers form?**
* **Answer:** **Differentiation**: When Earth was molten, dense iron/nickel sank to the core, and lighter silicates floated to the surface.

**Roughly, when did life first emerge?**
* **Answer:** ~3.8 Ga (Giga-annum/billion years ago).

**Roughly, when did photosynthesis begin?**
* **Answer:** ~2.5 - 3.0 Ga.

**What is the continuous habitable zone?**
* **Answer:** The region that remains habitable over the star's entire main-sequence lifetime.

**What is the faint, young Sun problem?**
* **Answer:** The early Sun was ~30% dimmer, yet Earth had liquid water. This was likely solved by a much stronger greenhouse effect (high $CO_2$ or $CH_4$).

**What are the basic layers of Earth's interior?**
* **Answer:** Inner Core (solid), Outer Core (liquid), Mantle, Crust.

**What are contributing factors to plate tectonics?**
* **Answer:** Mantle convection and tidal/radiogenic internal heating.

**Shield volcanoes vs. stratovolcanoes?**
* **Answer:** * **Shield:** Broad, flat (hotspots like Hawaii).
    * **Stratovolcanoes:** Steep, explosive (subduction zones like the Cascades).

**Archean atmosphere?**
* **Answer:** Reducing or neutral: high $N_2, CO_2, CH_4$, and **no $O_2$**.

**Banded-iron formations (BIFs)?**
* **Answer:** Layers of iron oxide in rock. They mark when $O_2$ produced by life reacted with dissolved iron in the oceans. **Chert** is the silica-rich rock often found in these layers.

### Earth Systems

**How has Earth's temperature varied?**
* **Answer:** It has fluctuated between "Greenhouse" (no ice) and "Icehouse" states. Over the last million years, it has oscillated between glacial and interglacial periods due to Milankovitch cycles.

**Why do we have seasons?**
* **Answer:** Earth's axial tilt (23.5 degrees).

**What are Milankovitch cycles?**
* **Answer:** Changes in Earth's orbit (eccentricity, tilt, and precession) that affect long-term climate and trigger ice ages.

**Solar cycle?**
* **Answer:** ~11-year cycle of magnetic activity (sunspots).

**What is a CME, Flare, Solar Wind?**
* **Answer:** * **Solar Wind:** Constant stream of charged particles.
    * **Flare:** Sudden burst of radiation.
    * **CME:** Massive burst of solar plasma and magnetic field.

**How is Earth's magnetic field generated?**
* **Answer:** Geodynamo: Convection of molten iron in the outer core. It is approximated as a dipole (like a bar magnet).

**Water cycle on Earth?**
* **Answer:** Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff. A water molecule stays in the atmosphere for about 9 days.

**Carbonate-silicate cycle?**
* **Answer:** $CO_2$ dissolves in rain $\rightarrow$ weathers rocks $\rightarrow$ washes into oceans $\rightarrow$ forms shells $\rightarrow$ subducted by tectonics $\rightarrow$ released by volcanoes. This acts as a thermostat.

**Evidence that CO2 increase is human-driven?**
* **Answer:** Isotopic ratios ($^{13}C$ depletion) show the $CO_2$ comes from burning ancient organic matter (fossil fuels), not volcanoes.

**What causes tides?**
* **Answer:** Differential gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun across the Earth.
    * **Spring Tides:** Sun and Moon aligned (highest tides).
    * **Neap Tides:** Sun and Moon at right angles (lowest tides).

### Mars

**What is the Martian Dichotomy?**
* **Answer:** The difference between the northern hemisphere (low, smooth, young plains) and southern hemisphere (high, cratered, old highlands).

**Primary regions?**
* **Answer:** Tharsis Bulge (volcanoes), Olympus Mons (largest volcano), Valles Marineris (giant canyon), Hellas Planitia (giant impact basin).

**Canali?**
* **Answer:** "Channels" observed by Schiaparelli, misinterpreted by Lowell as "canals" built by an advanced civilization. Mariner 4 and 9 showed they were optical illusions.

**Basic features?**
* **Answer:** Radius $\approx$ 1/2 Earth, Mass $\approx$ 1/10 Earth, Atmosphere $\approx$ 95% $CO_2$.

**Polar caps?**
* **Answer:** Mix of water ice and seasonal $CO_2$ ice (dry ice).

**Jeans Escape?**
* **Answer:** Atmospheric loss where individual molecules reach escape velocity due to thermal motion. Lighter molecules (H, He) escape more easily.
    * Calculate via: $v_{thermal} = \sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$. If $v_{thermal} \geq \frac{1}{6} v_e$, escape is significant.

**Martian Meteorites?**
* **Answer:** We know they are from Mars because they contain gas bubbles with the exact isotopic composition of the Martian atmosphere measured by Viking.

**Geological eons on Mars?**
* **Answer:** Noachian (wet/old), Hesperian (volcanic), Amazonian (cold/dry/current).

### Icy Worlds

**What are the Galilean moons?**
* **Answer:** Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto.

**What drives volcanism on Io?**
* **Answer:** Tidal heating from gravitational "flexing" by Jupiter and the other moons.

**Why Europa over Ganymede?**
* **Answer:** Europa's ocean is in direct contact with its rocky mantle (potential for hydrothermal energy). Ganymede's ocean is likely "sandwiched" between layers of ice.

**Enceladus interest?**
* **Answer:** Active geysers venting water and organic molecules from a subsurface ocean into space.

**Titan?**
* **Answer:** Only place besides Earth with stable liquid on its surface (methane/ethane lakes). It has a thick $N_2$ atmosphere.

### Extremophiles

* **Thermophile:** Heat lover.
* **Psychrophile:** Cold lover.
* **Acidophile:** Low pH lover.
* **Halophile:** Salt lover.
* **Piezophile:** High pressure lover.
* **Xerophile:** Dryness lover.
* **Tardigrades:** Relevant because they can survive the vacuum and radiation of space (cryptobiosis).
